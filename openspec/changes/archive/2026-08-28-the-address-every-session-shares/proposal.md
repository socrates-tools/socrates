## Why

The surface serves one page at a time.
`serve` binds `127.0.0.1:0`, so the operating system picks a port, and the whole server holds exactly one exchange.
Serving a second document does not open a second page: it revises the one that is already there.

That is fine while a person reviews one thing at a time and wrong the moment they do not.
Two reviews in two repositories are two rounds a person reads, and the second one silently replaces the first, so a page somebody was part-way through answering becomes a page about something else.
The first caller then polls a surface that is no longer serving what it served, and nothing anywhere says so.

The port is the other half of it.
A port the operating system chose is a different port every time, which is why an operator who puts anything in front of the surface has to configure a fixed one before it works at all, and why the address is unguessable to a person who wants to go back to a page they left open.
An operator who wants the page reachable configures one thing once; today the bind and the reach are two settings that have to agree, and the bind is the one that moves.

Both fall out of the same shape: one server, one exchange, one address.
A server that holds many exchanges and tells them apart by a path serves as many pages as there are rounds to read, on one address that never moves.

## What Changes

- **BREAKING**: the default bind becomes `127.0.0.1:18018` rather than `127.0.0.1:0`.
  A configured bind and `--address` override it exactly as they do now, and the loopback refusal is untouched.
- The surface holds many exchanges rather than one.
  Each is a session, served under its own path, with its own document, its own token and its own idle life.
- `serve` given no session mints one and prints the address of its page: the address the surface is reached at, then `/sessions/`, then the session it minted.
- `serve --session` names a session the surface already holds and revises its document, which is what serving a second document does today, now scoped to one page rather than to the server.
  A session the surface did not mint is refused rather than created, so `serve` without `--session` stays the one way a session comes into existence.
- `poll --session` and `end --session` name which page they are about.
- `stop` stays what it is: the server, not a session.
  `end` closes one exchange and the server keeps serving the rest.
- Idle collection becomes per-session, and the server stops when it holds none.
  One page nobody opened no longer takes down pages people are reading.
- The token becomes per-session, so a session's token answers for that session and no other.
- The page's three fixed request paths become relative to the session that served it.
- The skill says to carry the session `serve` printed through to `poll`.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

None.

### Modified Capabilities

- `review-surface`: `One binary, five commands, beside socrates` gains what a session is and how the five commands name one.
  `The surface binds a loopback address and nothing else` states the default bind as a fixed port rather than one the operating system chooses.
  `A surface answers only the session it was started for` becomes per-session rather than per-server.
  `Waiting for a person is bounded and says so when nobody comes` collects an idle session rather than shutting the server down, and stops the server when it holds no sessions.
  `A caller who writes a new document is asking again` applies to the named session.
- `agent-skill`: `The skill renders a round a person reads as a page` says the address `serve` prints holds the session, and that `poll` is given that session.

## Impact

- `internal/surface`: a registry of exchanges keyed by session, per-session tokens and documents, per-session idle collection, and routing under a session path.
  `Serve` stops being one document and becomes a server sessions are opened on.
- `internal/render`: the page carries the base its requests are made against, alongside the token and revision it already carries.
- `web/src/surface.js`: three absolute paths (`/answer`, `/`, `/live`) become relative to that base.
  The committed bundle is regenerated by `make assets`.
- `cmd/agora`: `--session` on `serve`, `poll` and `end`, and the minted session in what `serve` prints.
- `internal/skill`: the loop carries a session from `serve` to `poll`, and the flag check already holds that a flag the skill names exists.
- `internal/e2e`: two sessions served at once, answered independently, and neither disturbed by the other.
  The suite binds `127.0.0.1:0` explicitly where it must not take the fixed port.
- No new dependency.

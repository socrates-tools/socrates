# 37. A rejected alternative is recorded from the option the person declined

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-09-17
- Part of: socrates-design

## Decision

An ADR written by a design session carries a Rejected section built from the options the person did not choose, in the words they wrote beside the choice.

The common ADR format treats Rejected as optional, to be included "only when the rejected alternatives are worth remembering".
That is a concession to cost.
Reconstructing what was considered, after the interview that considered it, is expensive and unreliable, so the section is skipped and the next person reopens a settled question.

A design session has the alternatives already.
It offered them as options on a card, the choice came back naming one, and the reason came back in the person's own words.
So the section is a byproduct of the interview rather than a reconstruction of it.

An option nobody was offered never appears there.
The section records what was put to the person, which makes it bounded and checkable rather than a rationalisation written afterwards.

## Rejected

- Treating Rejected as optional, as the format this borrows from does.
  What makes it optional elsewhere is the cost of reconstruction, and that cost is not paid here.
- Recording the alternatives the session considered but did not offer.
  The record would then include reasoning nobody saw and nobody agreed to, which is the rationalisation the section exists to prevent.
- Keeping a transcript of the interview beside the ADR.
  What survives a design is what it decided, not what was said on the way, and a transcript invites deciding by search.

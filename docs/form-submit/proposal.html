agora surface · interaction proposal

The page becomes a form

A person answering a round should choose, type, and press one button. Today they press Commit this answer once per finding before the send button will carry anything, and an answer they forgot to commit is thrown away. This proposal removes committing entirely.

  • Changes the page and the client
  • Does not change the wire, the CLI, the skill
  • Files 3 sources, 9 test files
  • Settled 3 decisions, at the end

What answering a round costs now

A round of six findings is thirteen deliberate presses, seven of which exist only to tell the page that what it can already see is real. The page has a record of committed answers, and the send reads that record rather than the controls.

Same finding, same evidence, same options. What goes is the pair of buttons under the text field and the settled colour the card takes once it has been committed.

    What one press does

    The exchange already accepts more than one answer and already reopens when a new round is written to the same page. Submitting is what makes each of those a state the person can see.

    What this does not touch

    Committing is a habit of the client. It is not in the vocabulary, not in an ADR, and not on the wire. That is why this is a page change rather than a protocol change.

    Holds as it is

    • ✓surface.Sent, surface.Choice and surface.Answered keep every field. The browser posts the same JSON to the same route.
    • ✓agora poll prints the same thing, so the socrates-review skill needs no edit. It never mentions committing.
    • ✓No command is rendered on the page, and the surface still composes rather than runs.
    • ✓Delivery once, bounded waiting, collection per session, idle shutdown.
    • ✓A decision the new document drops loses its answer, exactly as the current re-render rule does.
    • ✓CONTEXT.md gains no word. "Commit" was never one of its terms.

    Goes

    • ×The Commit this answer and Take this answer back buttons, and the settled colour a committed card takes.
    • ×The committed map in the client, and commit, reopen and restore with it.
    • ×The discard warning. It asks about losing an uncommitted answer, and nothing is uncommitted any more.
    • ×The pair of send buttons as equals. One is the press, one is the exit.

    In its place: a completeness notice that names findings nobody answered, and a receipt of what went.

    Three things settled

    Each was put as what the person sees, with the mechanism in one line, and answered on the review page. These are the shapes to build.

    Q1

    What sits at the bottom of the page

    Today two equal buttons: Send and wait for a reply and Send and finish. A decent form has one obvious press.

    ✓One primary, one quiet secondarySettledThe person presses Send my answers. Send and finish stays beside it and stops looking like an equal choice. Mechanism: the same ends flag as today.
     One button onlyEnding offered on the receipt, after the answers had gone. Not taken.
     One button and a checkboxA "this is my last word" tickbox carrying the flag. Not taken.
    Q2

    Submitting with findings left blank

    A person may deliberately leave one to the session. Nothing is lost either way, so this is about completeness rather than data.

    ✓Say it once, then sendSettledThe first press names the blank findings with links to them. The next press sends. Mechanism: the notice replaces today's discard warning.
     Refuse until completeThe press disabled while anything is blank. Not taken: a finding left to the session is a legitimate answer.
     Send without askingThe counter as the only warning. Not taken.
    Q3

    What the page looks like once the answers have gone

    The exchange accepts a second answer today, so whichever of these was chosen, nothing on the wire changes.

    ✓A receipt, with a way backSettledControls go read only and a list says what was sent. Amend and send again reopens them. Mechanism: the disabled state that closing already uses.
     A receipt, and that is thatNo way back until a reply arrived. Not taken: a misclick needs somewhere to go.
     Stay editableNo locking at all. Not taken.

    Risks and what is still open

    A misclick now reaches the sessionA stray radio press used to be discarded unless it was committed. Under a form it goes. The completeness notice does not help, because a wrong answer is not a blank one. The receipt is what catches it, which is an argument for Q3 keeping a way back.
    Drafts across a re-renderToday only committed answers survive a reply being written to the page. A draft must now survive the same way, and a draft for a decision the new document does not hold must be dropped. That rule is already asserted for committed answers, so the scenario is rewritten rather than invented.
    A sticky bar over the evidenceThe bar sits at the bottom of the viewport on a long diff. The narrow-viewport scenario already asserts the evidence is not shrunk to nothing, and it will need to cover the bar not covering it either.
    Two rounds, one pageagora serve --session writes the next round into the page the person already has. If the receipt is still up when that lands, the receipt must give way to the new form rather than sit above it.
    This is not the specThe repository wants a change to start as a spec. This page is the proposal the decisions were taken on, and it sits beside the spec that is written from it. The ADR recording that the page is a form, and what per-answer committing gave up, belongs with that spec.

    Design source: agora's own system. The tokens, type scale, card, option and severity styles here are lifted from agora/web/src/surface.css, and the fonts are the IBM Plex faces the binary embeds, so the mockups show the real page rather than an impression of it.

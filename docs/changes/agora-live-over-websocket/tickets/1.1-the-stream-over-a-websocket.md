# 1.1: The stream over a WebSocket

## What to build

Every page holds its stream on its session's live route as one WebSocket instead of an `EventSource`, so the browser's six-connection limit no longer holds a send back however many pages are open.
The stream says what it says today, `revision`, `closed` and `uncollected`, in the same order and only on change, is told where the exchange stands when it connects, and counts as a reader of its session for exactly as long as the socket is open.
The surface opens a stream only for an upgrade whose `Origin` is its own, carries no token on it, and refuses a session it does not hold with not found before upgrading.
No `text/event-stream` route remains.
The new WebSocket module is accounted for in `NOTICE`, and agora's instructions describe the stream as it now is, with invariants for the origin check and the reader count.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] With seven pages open on one surface, a send from the last is collected by `socrates agora poll`.
- [ ] A revision served to an open page swaps in, ending the session closes the page, and the way back from a receipt goes once the answer is collected, all over the WebSocket.
- [ ] A WebSocket upgrade on the live route from another origin is refused, and one from the surface's own origin is accepted.
- [ ] An upgrade naming a session the surface does not hold is answered not found.
- [ ] A session with a page's WebSocket open is not collected for idleness, and one whose last WebSocket closed is.
- [ ] The live route no longer answers as an event stream.
- [ ] `NOTICE` names the WebSocket module and carries its licence in full.
- [ ] The browser suite records no policy violation on a page holding its stream.
